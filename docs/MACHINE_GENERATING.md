# Generating functions and actual binary64 execution

This chapter records DynaComp 0.10.0. It connects exact rational sequences and a supported algebraic series family to executable models, then accounts for the arithmetic used to run a reduction. It builds on the spectral source contracts of 0.9.0. It does not extend a finite-horizon machine guarantee to all future time.

## Exact generating functions as models

Write an ordinary generating function as F(z)=P(z)/Q(z), with coefficients in the rational numbers, Q(0) nonzero, and polynomial coefficients stored constant first. `RationalGF` cancels the polynomial gcd and scales Q(0) to one. Zero has the unique representation 0/1. Binary floats are rejected as polynomial coefficients: callers may supply exact rationals, or explicitly decode an existing float through `binary64.exact`.

For Q(z)=1+q_1 z+...+q_d z^d, coefficient comparison gives a_n=-sum_{j=1}^d q_j a_{n-j} once n exceeds the numerator degree. Before that point the numerator supplies the forcing terms. This distinction matters for finite transients. The autonomous sequence realization has dimension r=max(deg Q,deg P+1) for a nonzero reduced fraction, and dimension zero for the zero sequence. In particular, z/(1-z) describes 0,1,1,... and has r=2, while 1/(1-z) describes 1,1,1,... and has r=1. A finite polynomial of degree k requires k+1 autonomous states to retain its entire coefficient sequence.

The compiler initializes a shift-register state with a_0,...,a_{r-1}, reads its first coordinate, and advances using the recurrence, padded by zero coefficients for transient states. Its contract is a_n=C A^n x_0 for n>=0. This is a sequence realization; a transfer function with direct feedthrough uses a different convention. For x[t+1]=A x[t]+B u[t], the impulse contribution C A^n B occurs at t=n+1. Neither an input delay nor a direct output term is silently inferred here.

The packet includes the leading r-by-r Hankel determinant of the coefficient sequence. It is nonzero for the reduced rational fraction with this dimension. The realization supplies an upper bound r on Hankel rank, while this determinant supplies the matching lower bound: any s-state autonomous linear realization factors its Hankel matrix into observability and reachability matrices of inner dimension s. Thus the packet establishes minimality for this prescribed sequence, not an all-initial-state or every-input equivalence claim. Replay reconstructs the normalized fraction, state matrices, initial prefix and exact determinant; altered matrices, dimensions or witnesses are rejected.

`from_state` derives det(I-zA) by the exact Faddeev–LeVerrier trace recurrence, computes the numerator from the initial response, and cancels invisible factors. `from_recurrence` respects the existing chronological coefficient convention a[n+r]=sum c[i]a[n+i]. Sum, product, index weighting zF'(z), periodic prefixes, and arithmetic-progression extraction remain rational. Extraction uses A^stride and A^offset x_0, so it respects transients. `nth` uses binary matrix powering; the number of powering stages is logarithmic in the index, while integer sizes can still grow with the answer. Realization and state extraction are limited to 128 states.

## Analytic bounds, rather than an asymptotic promise

The books [Analytic Combinatorics](https://ac.cs.princeton.edu/home/) and [generatingfunctionology](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/DownldGF.html) motivate the separation of formal algebra, singularity structure and numerical remainders. Their texts are referenced, not redistributed. The new implementation supports two explicit remainder mechanisms; it is not a general singularity-analysis engine.

For a rational source, choose a positive rational R such that delta=1-sum_{j>=1}|q_j|R^j>0. The triangle inequality excludes denominator zeros on the closed disk. With M=sum |p_j|R^j/delta, Cauchy's inequality gives |a_n|<=M/R^n. At a real rational argument x with |x|<R, the omitted terms n>=N have absolute sum at most M(|x|/R)^N/(1-|x|/R). The certificate includes the source fraction, radius, majorant, count and exact rational bound. Failure of the disk test refuses this certificate; it does not prove a singularity exists there.

For positive rational alpha, the real branch (1-x)^(-alpha) has coefficients b_n=(alpha)_n/n! and b_{n+1}/b_n=(n+alpha)/(n+1). At 0<=x<1, all terms are nonnegative. After retaining n=0,...,N-1, every future term ratio is at most rho=x max(1,(N+alpha)/(N+1)). When rho<1, the remainder is at most b_N x^N/(1-rho). The partial sum and partial sum plus this remainder are rigorous lower and upper bounds. If rho>=1 the caller must retain more terms. The implementation does not attach a claimed finite realization to a nonrational binomial branch. The tests independently square the alpha=1/2 endpoints to bracket 1/(1-x), and check positive integer alpha against exact rational values.

## Treating binary64 words as integers

For sign s, exponent field E and fraction field F, a normal binary64 word represents (-1)^s(2^52+F)2^(E-1075). A subnormal represents (-1)^s F 2^(-1074). Fields E=2047 represent infinity or NaN and are excluded from arithmetic operands. Signed zero remains distinguishable in bit transport. These are the [standard binary formats](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.1); the implementation uses explicit big-endian transport rather than assuming host byte order.

`dyadic` removes powers of two from the integer significand. `exact` exposes the stored value as a rational. `round_bits` rounds an exact rational to nearest binary64, breaking ties toward an even significand. It handles the normal/subnormal boundary, underflow, carry into the exponent and overflow to infinity. An exact zero rounds to positive zero; negative nonzero underflow can produce negative zero. NaN payloads can be transported but are not ordered or treated as rational numbers. The ordered integer key reverses negative encodings and distinguishes the two zero words. `next_up` and `next_down` implement representable neighbors, with explicit infinity endpoints.

The dot-product kernel multiplies integer significands, aligns their dyadic exponents in an integer superaccumulator, and rounds only the final sum. It can cancel products whose individual hardware multiplications would overflow. It is not raw bitwise addition: the bit encoding is a representation, not the arithmetic value. In the retained example, sequential (10^16+1)-10^16 gives zero, while the exact-dot-then-round result is one. No hardware speed advantage is claimed; the portable Python kernel targets reproducibility and small critical reduced models.

## A guarantee for a specified machine protocol

The runtime first rounds the exact rational matrices E,G,H,D to binary64. It encodes x_0 using one exact dot and one rounding per row, advances each row using a single combined exact dot over Gz and Hu followed by one rounding, and rounds each output row after its exact dot. There are no BLAS calls, intermediate rounded products or undocumented FMA assumptions. Inputs are finite binary64 words, interpreted as their exact stored values. Their membership in the rational initial and per-time input boxes is checked exactly. An input such as 0.1 is outside the singleton rational box {1/10}; the guard does not widen the box to accommodate its nearby binary representation. The runtime copies its packet before replay and exposes a copy on inspection.

Let hats denote loaded matrices, X and U_t componentwise absolute domain bounds, Z_t bounds on the ideal rational reduced state, and e_t bounds on the componentwise difference between the machine state and that ideal state. The compiler uses |hat E-E|X plus the encoding roundoff budget for e_0. At each step it propagates

e_(t+1) <= |hat G|e_t + |hat G-G|Z_t + |hat H-H|U_t + roundoff_t,

where the exact pre-round row magnitude is bounded by |hat G|(Z_t+e_t)+|hat H|U_t. The output budget is |hat D|e_t+|hat D-D|Z_t plus its output rounding budget. For round-to-nearest-even, a safe absolute budget at magnitude M is 2^(-53)M+2^(-1075). The implementation rounds positive bounds upward on a 96-bit dyadic grid to keep receipts compact. This includes subnormal errors. Exact zero magnitude has zero rounding budget. If a matrix load overflows or a row's domain magnitude cannot be bounded by the largest finite binary64 value, certification fails closed. Cancellation may make an individual real execution safe even when this conservative domain test refuses it.

`certify_execution` bounds uniform machine deviation from supplied rational reduced dynamics. `compose_execution` additionally replays a validated finite-model packet, or a spectral-composition packet, and adds the source/model budget at every output time. It preserves any requested contraction check. A combined upper bound exceeding the tolerance is `not_certified`, not a refutation. The composed certificate is tied to its source matrices and boxes; there is no public facility to inject an arbitrary asserted model-error number.

The retained infinite heat-rod example uses the 64-mode sampled source and its three-state projection. Over 20 samples its source/model worst bound is approximately 0.004665714822446214. The uniform execution-only worst bound is approximately 1.1927634472226466e-15. Adding time-matched bounds gives 0.004665714822446697, still below 0.01. This covers the specified stored-input domain and exact-dot machine protocol. It does not certify the separate NumPy prepared backend, all-time rounded execution, arbitrary decimal sensor truth, continuous-time intersample projection error, or physical calibration.

## Reproduction and remaining work

Run `python examples/machine_generating.py` after installing the package. It regenerates replayable GF, algebraic-tail and machine packets under `receipts/machine_generating`. The CLI accepts `rational_gf`, `recurrence_gf`, `state_gf`, `rational_gf_tail`, `binomial_tail`, `binary64_power`, `binary64_execution` and `machine_composition`. All example specifications are compiled, JSON-round-tripped and replayed by the existing regression test.

Randomized tests compare thousands of words and exact-rational roundings against independent host conversions, exercise ties and boundary encodings, compare integer dot accumulation against Fraction products, and bracket independently simulated rational trajectories with machine-error certificates. All-time machine invariants, a compiled superaccumulator backend, efficient certified conventional dot products, wider singularity families and validated descriptor systems remain priorities. These require their own derivations and benchmark evidence.

Portable generating-function arithmetic additionally uses a 16384-bit value budget and at most 10000 retained coefficients. Large-index growing sequences refuse oversized arithmetic rather than attempting to allocate an unbounded answer. These limits do not change the exact mathematical identities.

Release verification: all 210 DynaComp tests pass with numerical extras. The stdlib-only run completes with 192 passing and 18 numerical tests skipped. Existing example receipts regenerate unchanged. The retained machine/source example replays independently. These runs use Python 3.12 locally; the workflow covers Python 3.10, 3.12 and 3.13.
